# Assertion and Knowledge Writing Rules

## Statement phrasing

- Begin with what holds or what should be done, without a preamble.
- Write as natural declarative prose; do not prefix the statement with a Subject or Type name.
- Express the strength of a rule through its wording.
- Use words such as "always" and "must" only for rules that permit no exception.
- Express defaults that permit exceptions with wording such as "by default," "normally," or "unless there is a specific reason otherwise."
- Do not prefix a statement with classification labels such as `Absolute:` or `Default:`.

## Compound Knowledge statements

- Write a single proposition as concise prose.
- For multiple items on the same axis, use a short lead sentence followed by a Markdown bullet list.
- Do not force environment-specific values, alternatives, or mappings into one long sentence joined by conjunctions.
- Do not put headings inside a Statement.

## Rationale phrasing

- Explain the reason directly instead of repeating the conclusion.
- Do not prefix the text with labels such as `Reason:` or `Rationale:`.
- Keep a short reason in one paragraph.
- Use a bullet list only when several reasons have a clear parallel relationship.
