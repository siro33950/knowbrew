# Common Writing Rules

## Accuracy

- Assert only what the available evidence establishes.
- Do not turn inference, possibility, or uncertainty into certainty without evidence.
- Preserve conditions, exceptions, scope, and distinctions between concepts.
- Do not merge different concepts or separate problems as if they were the same.
- Do not reduce multiple causes to a single cause.
- When stating causation, include the mechanism or reason supported by the evidence.
- Do not add reasons, examples, comparisons, or evaluations that are absent from the evidence.

## Structure

- Give each paragraph one topic.
- Use the first sentence of a paragraph to identify its topic.
- State each point once; do not repeat it in different words.
- Use terminology, classifications, and conceptual roles consistently throughout the output.
- Do not introduce a concept or document name before it is needed.
- Present information in an order that makes relationships traceable; do not leave a topic and return to it later.

## Reader effort

- Omit identifiers, filenames, function names, and numbers that are unnecessary for understanding or later reference.
- Do not add details or examples that are not used later.
- Identify a referent explicitly when context does not determine it uniquely.
- Write so the reader can follow relationships without rereading earlier text.
- Omit intermediate explanation that the intended reader can derive without help.
- Shorten text when doing so does not change its meaning.

## Presentation and style

- Write in the language and style required by the user's configuration.
- Use sentence and paragraph lengths and structures that are natural in the output language.
- Do not pack multiple claims or relationships into one sentence.
- Follow the output language's conventions for punctuation, quotation, emphasis, and separators.
- Use the same term for the same referent; avoid unnecessary variation.
- State the substance directly instead of repeating transitions or rhetorical framing.
- Omit previews, recaps, and emphasis that add no content.
- Do not invent dialogue with the reader or use metaphors whose meaning is unclear.
- Do not weaken established facts without a reason.
- Do not state uncertain content as certain merely to make the prose smoother.
